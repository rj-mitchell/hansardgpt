Changelog

What has been added, changed or fixed, and why. The newest changes come first.

13 October 2026

  • Briefings open with an “In brief” summary, favour specific points (the measure, figure, bill or date) over generalities everyone shares, and no longer begin with remarks about the records. Statements without a source are counted under the briefing, and can be shown, rather than highlighted.
  • Follow-up questions: suggested follow-ups appear under a briefing, and a follow-up such as “What have peers said about this?” keeps the earlier topic and filters.
  • Questions about MPs for city, town or rural seats now search only those seats. The devolved parliaments and witnesses' evidence to committees are searched by default, and each briefing says what was searched.
  • MP and peer pages load faster and in stages, list five contributions at a time without “Will the Member give way?” interventions, and show membership and all-party groups first. Former MPs and peers since 1997 can be found by name.
  • New example questions; Settings sits beside the question box; This week is second in the menu; on a phone, Back (or “Back to the briefing”) closes a source.

12 October 2026

  • Faster: searches read only the records they need, the search models load when the site starts, and pages that change only with the twice-daily update are kept ready in memory. Questions that name a member (such as "What did Theresa May say about her Brexit deal?") work again, and a name like "Benn" now finds Hilary Benn.
  • A comparison chosen on the Compare page now starts straight away, labelled as a comparison. The menu is the same on every page, with the features at the top and pages about the site at the foot.
  • The Ask page now says plainly that briefings are written by AI, what that adds and how it differs from a keyword search; the MPs and peers page lists everything shown about each member; links to follow pages in a feed reader have moved to the foot of each page.
  • Early day motions now go back to 1997, with who signed each one; and MPs' majorities at the 1997, 2001 and 2005 general elections are shown, from the House of Commons Library.
  • Written statements can now be read on the site, with the minister's role on the day; "This week" links to each one. Timelines now show the newest first. The menu keeps "Ask a question" on every page and puts the site's main features first. Member pages show registered interests, groups, donations and company roles open; the full lists of MPs and peers open when you choose.
  • "This week" now says when Parliament is in recess, when each House last sat and how many written answers have been published since, above the last week it sat.
  • Company roles: the companies where MPs and peers are (or were) directors, secretaries or partners, from the Companies House register, on each member's page and on a new company roles page. Records are matched by name and month and year of birth; companies also named in the member's register of interests are marked.
  • The record now starts at the 1997 general election, not 2010: debates, votes, written answers and statements, Westminster Hall and Standing Committees, with each speaker's party, seat, region and role on the day. The Lords record starts in November 1999. Election results and early day motions still start in 2010.
  • How parties whipped a vote, where TheyWorkForYou Votes has reported it: shown beside the vote and given to briefings. Members voting differently from colleagues in a reported free vote are no longer counted as rebelling.
  • Every register of interests: peers' pages now show the Register of Lords' Interests, and members of the Scottish Parliament, the Senedd and the Northern Ireland Assembly their own parliament's register, each with what has changed over the past year.
  • Donations to MPs: political donations registered with the Electoral Commission since 2001, on each MP's page and searchable by donor. Donations to an MP's local party are shown separately. Each links to the Commission's own record; see the methodology for how they are matched.
  • All-party groups: every all-party parliamentary group (and the devolved parliaments' cross-party groups), with its officers, members, secretariat and registered funders, and each MP's and peer's groups on their page. Funders can be looked up across groups.
  • Links to other free sites wherever they do something this one does not: Hansard's own search for every mention of the words in a question, TheyWorkForYou for alerts and records before 2010, each member's official profile, full voting record and WriteToThem, each bill's documents and the resulting Act, and Parliament's calendar of coming business. The About page now lists what is not covered yet and where to look meanwhile.

11 October 2026

  • MP pages show what has changed in their register of interests over the past year: entries that are new and entries no longer listed, compared with the edition published a year earlier. Bill pages have an “Ask about this stage” link on each sitting. When a speaker declared an interest in what they said (for example “I refer the House to my entry in the Register”), the source now shows that sentence.
  • Briefings can now include what witnesses told select committees (from 2022): turn it on under Settings. Witnesses' answers are kept in their own section and labelled as evidence to a committee, never as the view of the committee or of Parliament. Inquiry pages now show their committee for every inquiry, including bill and European scrutiny work.
  • For researchers and developers: a free search API and the site's other data as JSON, documented on Data and API. A press and contact page explains how to cite the site and offers logo files.
  • Devolved parliaments: read every sitting of the Scottish Parliament, the Senedd and the Northern Ireland Assembly since 2021, and search what their members said, with each member's party and seat on the day. Members' pages show their seats and recent contributions there. Briefings still cover Westminster only. On the Charts page, “Across the UK's parliaments” compares how often a subject is raised in the Commons, the Lords and the three devolved parliaments. Briefings can include the devolved parliaments too: tick “Devolved parliaments” under Settings, and their members' contributions are reported separately, by parliament.
  • Committees: every committee's inquiries, reports and the government's responses (and how long they took), and who gave evidence at each session. The witness index shows which organisations committees hear from, how often and to whom. Ministers' and other members' pages list the sessions where they gave evidence. Transcripts of evidence sessions since 2022 can be read on the site, turn by turn, and searched.
  • MP pages now show the member's entries in the latest Register of Members' Financial Interests: jobs and earnings, donations, gifts, visits, property and shareholdings, as registered. Former MPs show their entries in the last edition before they left.
  • Devolution notes: when a question is about a devolved subject, such as health, schools or housing, a note beside the briefing says which nations Westminster decides it for. See the methodology.
  • Early day motions: search the motions MPs put down, read them and see who signed, with each member's party on the day. MP pages list the motions they tabled and signed. They are being collected from Parliament a year at a time, so older motions will appear over the coming days. Briefings use the motions most relevant to a question as evidence, with signatures counted by party; this can be switched off under “Include”.
  • This week: what happened in Parliament each sitting day, with every debate, statement and vote, and the weeks before it back to 2010.
  • Briefings can be downloaded as a Word document, with numbered citations and the full list of sources, or printed and saved as a PDF.
  • “Copy embed code” puts a briefing's preview card, linked to the briefing, on a website or blog.
  • Debate pages: every debate since 2010 can now be read in full on the site, in order, with each speaker's party, seat and role on the day and the votes where they happened. Sources, timelines, member pages and bill pages link straight to the place in the debate.
  • RSS feeds, to follow a member, the topic briefings, bills, this changelog or the corrections in a feed reader. Look for “Follow … in a feed reader” at the foot of each page.
  • “Ask about this” in the source panel: a ready-made follow-up question about the speaker or the subject of any source, which you can change before asking.
  • Every vote now says what kind of vote it was (an amendment, a timetable motion, an amendment to decline a bill's second reading and so on) and what voting either way usually means, read from the motion recorded before it. Shown on bill pages, member pages, timelines and in briefings' sources. Briefings are told not to treat a vote on an amendment or a procedural motion as a vote on the whole bill. A new glossary explains these and other parliamentary terms. For about 1,600 votes, a plain-English description of what voting aye or no meant is shown too, from TheyWorkForYou Votes.
  • New pages: Bills (each bill's journey through Parliament, with its votes), Charts (how often a subject is raised, by party, House and nation), Compare (parties, members or nations side by side) and Timelines (what a member or party said on a topic, and how they voted, in date order). A new logo: handwriting on one page, the same words found by search on the other.
  • Every quotation in a briefing is now checked word for word against the speech it is attributed to, and marked if it cannot be found. Briefings now back each summary of a group's view with a quoted example, and gather notes about the evidence into a closing paragraph.
  • Topic briefings: where MPs and peers stand on sixteen issues Parliament keeps returning to, from the cost of living to defence, each on its own page and kept up to date as new debates arrive. Earlier versions are kept. A selection of readers' briefings is featured there too.
  • A page for every MP and peer since 2010: seats and majorities, roles over time, recent contributions and voting record, with rebellions. Find your MP by postcode.
  • The daily limit on new briefings is now a reminder instead of a barrier: every ten new briefings, a short note explains what the site costs and how to support it. You can carry on either way.
  • The site is now clearly marked as in beta, and a new other resources page lists other sites for researching Parliament and what each does best.
  • Each MP's majority at the time they spoke, and now, from Parliament's election results. Questions can compare, or be limited to, MPs in marginal and safe seats, or first-term and re-elected MPs. See Methodology.
  • "Sources only" shows the balanced evidence without writing a briefing. When a daily limit is reached, the sources are still shown.
  • A note when most of the evidence comes from one party.
  • Links to the site's open-source code.

10 October 2026

  • New About, privacy and terms pages. Briefings may be reused under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 licence. Visitor codes now change daily, and requests are no longer logged.
  • Accessibility improvements (a skip link, larger tick boxes, clearer screen reader announcements and stronger contrast), and an accessibility statement.
  • "Report a problem" under every briefing, and a public corrections page listing errors put right and briefings withdrawn.
  • Questions can compare women and men, or members of different ages, or be limited to one group ("what have younger MPs said about housing?"). Age is the member's age on the day they spoke. See Methodology.
  • The site is now called Where They Stand. Every briefing is labelled as written by AI from the cited Hansard records.
  • Briefings can be copied with their sources, or as a list of references in the usual Hansard style (for example, "HC Deb 9 March 2023, col 412").
  • Each briefing shows the balance of its evidence: speaker groups, parties and kinds of seat. Its sources are visible while it is being written.
  • Briefings must use neutral reporting words. Judgemental words such as "misled" or "U-turn" may appear only inside quotations, and any that slip into the briefing's own voice are underlined.
  • Briefings are written in British English. Quotations are never altered.
  • A "Was this briefing useful?" question, and visit counts that use no cookies.
  • New pages: Methodology, this changelog and Status.
  • Every briefing now has its own permanent address, with a preview card showing the question when the link is shared. Saved briefings show when they were prepared and the most recent record they drew on.
  • A new mark: the bound record, open, with a ribbon marking the place. Shared links now show a preview card, and the site can be added to a phone's home screen.

9 October 2026

  • First version: the Hansard record since May 2010, searched by words and by meaning, with sourced briefings grouped by who holds each position, and votes alongside.
  • Seats are classed as city, town or rural by the size of the urban area people live in, using official data from the ONS, National Records of Scotland and NISRA, not by official city status.
  • The site is open to everyone, with no sign-in, and costs are protected by daily limits instead.
  • A design based on the printed Official Report.
  • Stricter checks on citations, and clearer warnings for statements without a source.
